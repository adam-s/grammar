## Central generalization

**A sentence diagram is a map of nested relationships. At each layer, identify
the unit's form and head, then identify the job that whole unit has in the next
larger layer.** Heads license complements; modifiers add information to a head
or a larger phrase; a clause can itself fill a role inside another clause; and
coordination relates equal units without making either one the other's head.

This is why a diagram is more than a list of parts of speech. The same form can
perform different functions, and the same function can be filled by different
forms. In _The inspector who arrived reported that the wiring smoked_, the
relative clause modifies _inspector_ inside the subject noun phrase, while the
nominal clause is what _reported_ takes in the outer predicate. Both are
clauses, but they have different relationships to the larger structure.

The course's labels and tree shapes are its teaching analysis, not a claim that
all grammars use the same categories or draw every relation at the same level.
The durable result for a learner is to explain **why a span belongs together
and what it does where it sits**, rather than to recite a label.

## Evidence, not a diagnostic checklist

The capstone should teach a way to assemble evidence. Transformations and
paraphrases test consequences of a proposed analysis; they do not define form,
function, headedness, or intended meaning. A successful test is evidence in the
construction where it works. A failed test can reflect context, style, lexical
choice, or a mismatch between the test and the proposed span.

| Evidence to use when relevant                       | What it can support in this course                                                                                                                           | Limit to state beside it                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Find the head and its dependents.                   | The noun _inspector_ groups with its relative clause in c40-d; the verb _reported_ groups with its clausal complement.                                       | “Head” is a structural relation, not “the most important word.” Coordination has no ordinary head in the course model.                                                                  |
| Substitute a whole phrase or clause.                | _The inspector reported it_ can support the outer object boundary in c40-d; a full NP replacement can expose the NP boundary in the alternate c40-a reading. | Replacement is category- and context-sensitive. It supports a proposed constituent; it does not select one analysis when two readings remain possible or make an embedded clause an NP. |
| Remove a candidate modifier or supplement.          | Removing _before the season ended_ from c40-b, or _Fortunately_ from c40-i, leaves a complete outer frame.                                                   | A removable unit may still contribute important meaning. Removal does not distinguish every modifier from every complement or prove an attachment.                                      |
| Compare a controlled paraphrase or active relation. | _Someone dredged the harbour_ can make the passive relation in c40-b visible; two paraphrases state the two readings of c40-a.                               | Rewording can add an unstated agent, tense, focus, or meaning. It explains a proposed reading; it is not an independent definition.                                                     |
| Use agreement and finite form.                      | _The wiring smoked_ and _the deeds vanished_ make the inner subject–predicate frames in c40-d and c40-e easier to find.                                      | Agreement is uneven in English and does not show where the whole clause attaches or what function it has.                                                                               |
| Use movement only where it remains natural.         | Fronting an event PP can support a clause-level reading in a suitable context.                                                                               | Information structure, style, and lexical choice constrain movement. A failed movement test does not prove noun attachment.                                                             |
| Preserve two readings when both analyses fit.       | c40-a records two accepted structures, as does fix-ambiguous.                                                                                                | Context may make one reading intended, but no context is stored with the bare practice sentence. The canonical storage order is not a linguistic verdict.                               |

A learner-facing procedure can therefore be short and honest:

1. Mark the outer frame or frames, then find the head of each unit you open.
2. Ask what each completed unit does in the next larger frame: required
   complement, modifier, clause role, coordinate, or material outside the
   frame.
3. Use the one or two tests that fit that proposed relation. Explain what the
   result supports.
4. If two complete, ordinary analyses survive, keep both and ask whether
   supplied context chooses one.

This retains the course's verb-first entry without turning one diagnostic into a
definition or pretending that all relations are discovered in the same order.

## Revision direction

1. **Lead with nested relationships, not a list of labels.** Open with form,
   outer function, head, and dependency as four connected questions. Use
   _The inspector who arrived reported that the wiring smoked_ in prose to
   contrast two clauses with different jobs, then choose or build a non-graded
   fixture that visibly shows the same architecture.
2. **Make form versus function the organizing distinction.** State that a
   relative clause and a nominal clause share form but not outer function; that
   a PP can be an adverbial or noun-internal modifier; and that a clause can be
   a subject, object, modifier, or coordinate. Do not reopen every earlier
   definition.
3. **Replace the universal diagnostic demand with converging evidence.** Keep
   the outer-frame-first procedure, but make the test conditional: use
   replacement, removal, agreement, movement, or paraphrase only when it bears
   on the relation in question. Put the limit beside each example.
4. **Repair the fixture evidence before revising captions.** Fix-ambiguous and
   fix-coordinated-nominal cannot currently show both readings in the static
   lesson. Add a way for lesson data to name a reading, or add approved
   single-reading fixtures for each side of a two-tree comparison. Until then,
   do not claim the figures visibly show two attachments or two scopes.
5. **Replace or demote the across-the-board figure.** It is a valuable model
   fixture but not final practice and depends on question extraction and two
   indexed gaps that the ten sentences do not review. Use it only as an
   explicitly bounded further-dependency example, or replace it with an
   approved fixture that composes relations practised in lesson 40.
6. **Treat final practice as integration, not coverage.** Keep its opening
   variety, ambiguity, passive, non-finite clauses, coordination, and
   supplement. Do not claim that it tests every lesson. If practice changes,
   replace one duplicate relative-plus-nominal entry with a comparative or a
   controlled complement/modifier contrast.
7. **Close on explanation, not certainty.** A finished diagram is an argument
   because each boundary and label states a relationship and has appropriate
   evidence behind it. Some arguments end in two accepted trees; that is a
   result, not a failure to finish.

## Sources opened and read

- Anderson, Bjorkman, Denis, Doner, Grant, Sanders, and Taniguchi,
  _Essentials of Linguistics_, second edition, [§6.14, “Trees: Introducing
  X-bar theory”](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/chapter/x-bar-theory-introduction/).
  Read in full. It treats phrases as headed structures, describes the closer
  head–complement relation, and uses replacement and ellipsis evidence for
  internal phrase structure. Its X-bar notation is not the app's required
  notation, but its account supports the dossier's nested-relations claim.
- The same textbook, [§6.6, “Clausal
  embedding”](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/chapter/sentences-inside-sentences-embedded-clauses/).
  Read in full. It shows that a clause can occur as a verb's complement,
  explains that verbs select the kinds of complements they take, and treats
  finite and non-finite embedding as distinct possibilities. It supports the
  c40-d/c40-j analysis while warning against a single surface test for all
  clauses.
- Department for Education, [_English glossary_](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7c8e4ded915d48c24108e2/English_Glossary.pdf),
  pp. 2–3, 9–13, and 16–17. Read the cited pages. The official school reference defines
  a phrase through its head, says phrases may contain other phrases,
  distinguishes a unit's form from uses such as adverbial, and recognizes finite
  and non-finite clauses. Its treatment is a pedagogical framework, not a
  reason to present every label as theory-neutral.
- Anderson et al., _Essentials of Linguistics_, [§6.4, “Identifying phrases:
  constituency tests”](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/chapter/identifying-phrases-constituency-tests/).
  The publisher's indexed text was read; direct page opening returned 403. It
  presents replacement, movement, clefting, and question-answer tests as
  evidence for proposed constituents, warns that coordination is not always
  reliable, and requires attention to both grammaticality and meaning. It
  supports treating transformations as converging evidence rather than
  definitions.

## Rejected or qualified claims

- **“The final lesson teaches nothing new, so it only needs longer sentences.”**
  Reject. Its job is to make previously separate relations work together;
  length alone adds no structural decision.
- **“Every label needs a transformation.”** Reject. Different relationships
  have different evidence, and a real ambiguity can remain after all relevant
  diagnostics.
- **“The fixture diagrams show both readings.”** Reject. The page currently
  renders the canonical reading only, while one fixture stores only one of the
  proposed scope readings.
- **“The final practice covers the whole course.”** Reject. It is broad but
  omits several taught constructions and repeats a relative-plus-nominal
  pattern.
- **“A final diagram proves the author's intended meaning.”** Reject. The model
  can record and grade a defensible analysis; context and human review are still
  needed to establish an intended reading or naturalness.
