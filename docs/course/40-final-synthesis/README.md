## What the lesson decides

**Nothing new.** Everything, once, together.

## The finding

**Seven distinct asked-shapes, second only to lesson 15's nine.** Picks run 29
to 45, the widest range in the course, and 8.9 words a sentence.

As a closing lesson it works. It composes a relative clause with a nominal
clause, with an object complement, with two objects, with a required adverbial:

| Sentence                                                          | What it combines              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| _The inspector who arrived late reported that the wiring smoked._ | relative + nominal            |
| _The surveyor who objected called the boundary wrong._            | relative + object complement  |
| _The tenant who complained twice sent her landlord a notice._     | relative + two objects        |
| _The guard who waited outside put that ledger in the safe._       | relative + required adverbial |

That is real composition, and it is the only lesson besides 15 and 16 that does
it deliberately.

## The one thing that is uniform

**Every sentence begins the same way: a noun phrase with a relative clause
modifying its subject.** _The inspector who…_, _The surveyor who…_, _The clerk
who…_, ten times.

So the second element varies and the first never does. A learner can start every
sentence in the lesson the same way and only has to think from the main verb on.

Also missing from the synthesis: no passive, no participial clause, no gerund, no
infinitive, no coordination, no comparative. Six of the seven things Stage 5
taught are absent from the lesson that is supposed to gather everything.

Verb-final in 5 of 10.

## What a synthesis lesson should be

The measure is not length. It is whether a learner can predict the shape before
reading, and here they can predict the first half of every one.

The two most valuable additions:

1. **Vary the opening.** Put the relative clause on an object instead of a
   subject, or start with a fronted adverbial clause.
2. **Include Stage 5.** A synthesis that omits the passive, the participial and
   the gerund is a synthesis of Stage 4.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                                            | What defeats it                       | In the course?     |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| The sentence opens with a relative-modified subject | any other opening                     | **no** — 10/10     |
| The synthesis covers everything taught              | six Stage 5 constructions are absent  | **no**             |
| Longer means harder                                 | lesson 39 is longer and has one shape | **yes** — usefully |

## What this should change

1. **Add the missing constructions.** Passive, participial, gerund, infinitive
   and coordination are all taught and none is here.
2. **Vary the first four words.**
3. **One two-reading sentence.** Lesson 27 is the only lesson with ambiguity, and
   a final synthesis is the natural second place for it.
