# Lesson 21 — Modifiers after the head

Researched 28 August 2026. An author's dossier. See
[../01-introduction/README.md](../01-introduction/README.md).

## What the lesson decides

| Decision            | In plain words                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| `func:postmodifier` | it sits after the head and narrows it |

This is the label lesson 4's sentences needed. _The man in the coat_ has been in
the corpus since lesson 4 and could not be drawn until now.

## The finding

Five distinct asked-shapes and good frame reuse — the postmodified phrase
appears as a subject, an object, an indirect object and a subject complement.
That is real composition.

Two things are missing.

**No nesting.** Measured: the deepest prepositional-phrase nesting inside a
lesson-21 noun phrase is zero. Every postmodifier is a flat `P + NP`. Lesson 19
nests two deep after a verb; lesson 21 never nests inside a noun phrase, which
is where nesting is unambiguous and therefore safest to teach.

**Verb-final in 5 of 10**, the highest in Stage 3 outside lesson 26.

## The tests

**Agreement.** The head controls the verb across the postmodifier: _The lock on
the shed **was** rusty_, not _were_. This is the test that makes the postmodifier
visible and it is the one that matters in real writing, where the nearest noun
pulls the verb wrongly. It is named in
[../05-find-the-head/README.md](../05-find-the-head/README.md) and appears
nowhere in the app.

**Omission.** _The path beyond the gate narrowed_ → _The path narrowed_. What
drops is the postmodifier; what cannot drop is the head.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                                              | What defeats it                                         | In the course?             |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| A prepositional phrase after a noun is a postmodifier | _She read the report on the train_ — could be adverbial | **no** — that is lesson 27 |
| The head is the noun before the preposition           | true here, and it is the point                          | yes                        |
| One postmodifier per phrase                           | _the key to the door of the shed_                       | **no**                     |

## What this should change

1. **Nest one postmodifier inside another.** It is the safest nesting in English
   and lesson 19 already establishes the depth.
2. **Name the agreement test.** Lesson 21 is where it becomes load-bearing.
