# Lesson 14 — When an adverbial is required · sentences

Drafted 28 August 2026. See [README.md](README.md).

**This is the most clearly broken lesson in Stage 2.** Measured: all ten of its
adverbials are obligatory and none is optional, so the removal test — the whole
point of the lesson — has nothing to reject. And every verb is a form of _be_,
so the required adverbial is only ever shown in one frame. The first optional
adverbial in the entire course arrives at lesson 16.

The fix is matched pairs, the same design lesson 20 already uses successfully.

## Sentences

| #   | Sentence                                 | The step                                            |
| --- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | The keys are on the table.               | _be_ plus a place, which cannot be removed          |
| 2   | The museum is beside the river.          | the same frame, so the pattern is visible           |
| 3   | She put the milk in the fridge.          | **a verb that is not _be_**, and an object too      |
| 4   | The path leads through the woods.        | a required place with no object                     |
| 5   | The children played outside.             | **an optional adverbial** — the first in the lesson |
| 6   | She placed the box under the bench.      | required                                            |
| 7   | She opened the box under the bench.      | **the same words, optional**                        |
| 8   | The ladder is against the wall.          | back to _be_ after the pair                         |
| 9   | The driver waited at the depot.          | optional                                            |
| 10  | The driver kept the engine at the depot. | **the same place phrase, required**                 |

## Notes

Items 6 and 7 differ by one word, and that word changes whether the phrase can
be removed. _She opened the box._ is a sentence; _\*She placed the box._ is not.
Items 9 and 10 do the same with a different verb pair.

Items 3 and 4 add the frames the built lesson omits entirely: a verb requiring
both an object and a place, and a verb requiring only a place.

**Six decisions arrive in this lesson** — `P`, `PP`, `Adv`, `AdvP`, `adverbial`
and `complement` — which is the largest jump in the course. Four of them are word
classes rather than the lesson's actual idea, and lessons 18 and 19 later teach
nothing new precisely because it all arrived here. Whether to split the lesson is
a course-outline question, not a sentence one.

Item 5 is the only single-word adverbial. That is enough for the removal test and
it keeps the token spread inside the ceiling.
