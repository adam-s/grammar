# Lesson 35 — Participial clauses

Researched 28 August 2026. An author's dossier. See
[../01-introduction/README.md](../01-introduction/README.md).

**Status:** This dossier measures the built corpus. [sentences.md](sentences.md) proposes replacements that are not yet parsed or accepted as course data.

## What the lesson decides

| Decision          | In plain words                                       |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `fin:participial` | the clause is built on a participle and has no tense |

## The finding

**All ten are past participles sitting after a noun.** _The engine repaired after
the flood failed. The ledger audited by the inspector vanished. Those deeds filed
under the counter burned._

Two distinct asked-shapes, eight of ten verb-final, and the picks are the highest
in the course so far at 35.2.

Two shapes are missing and both were checked against **both** corpora:

**No present participle ever postmodifies a noun.** _The child standing by the
gate waved._ A postmodifying clause in this project is only ever
`participial/relative` or a finite relative, never a present participle.

**No participial clause ever functions as an adverbial.** _Damaged by the flood,
the bridge closed._ The fronted participial is the shape that actually confuses
readers, and it is the one lesson 39 would most want.

Both are corpus gaps rather than model gaps. The construction probe builds both
cleanly; they still need fixtures and accepted sentence parses.

## The tests

**The gap.** _The engine repaired after the flood_ has no subject inside the
participial clause; the engine outside fills it. Same machinery as lesson 31.

**Expansion to a full relative.** _The engine **that was** repaired after the
flood failed._ If the words can be put back and the sentence survives, the
participial is a reduced relative. This is the definitive test and it costs
nothing.

**No tense.** _repaired_ does not shift when the sentence does. The main verb
_failed_ does.

## The hard part

**Eight of these ten sentences are garden paths.** _The engine repaired…_ reads
as a main clause with _repaired_ as its verb until _failed_ arrives and forces a
re-read. That is the same effect as the classic _The horse raced past the barn
fell_, which is in the fixtures.

This is a real difficulty and it is not marked anywhere. The learner is not told
that the first reading is meant to fail; they are simply asked to build.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                                  | What defeats it         | In the course?                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| The participle is the main verb           | this whole lesson       | **yes** — that is the difficulty |
| A participial clause is a past participle | a present participle    | **no** — corpus gap              |
| A participial clause modifies a noun      | a fronted adverbial one | **no** — corpus gap              |

## What this should change

1. **Add fixtures for a present-participle postmodifier and a fronted
   participial adverbial.** The construction probe proves that the model holds
   both; the corpora do not.
2. **Name the expansion test.** _that was_ put back is the cleanest evidence in
   the lesson.
