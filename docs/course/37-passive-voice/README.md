## The finding: this lesson is well built

**Four distinct asked-shapes**, and the range of picks (16 to 26) is the widest
in Stage 5. It covers more of its own construction than most lessons do:

| Sentence                                      | What it shows                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| _The harbour was dredged by the contractors._ | with an agent                |
| _Those deeds were filed._                     | without one                  |
| _The guest was given a key._                  | the indirect object promoted |
| _The driver was considered reliable._         | an object complement kept    |

Those last two are the hard cases and both are here. Lesson 12's indirect object
and lesson 13's object complement both come back and behave differently, which
is the composition the rest of the course keeps failing to do.

## Why this lesson matters more than its position suggests

**This is where the lesson-1 shortcut finally breaks.** The app's hint for the
subject is _WHO or WHAT does it?_, and _The harbour was dredged by the
contractors_ is the first sentence in the entire course where the subject does
nothing. Thirty-six lessons of a rule that always worked, and it stops here.

See [../01-introduction/README.md](../01-introduction/README.md) and
[../03a-doer-and-subject/](../03a-doer-and-subject/), which plants the doubt
early precisely so that this lesson can collect on it.

## The tests

**Turn it back.** _The contractors dredged the harbour._ If the sentence can be
reversed with the subject becoming an object, it was passive.

**The agent is optional and the object is not.** _Those deeds were filed_ has no
agent and is complete; the active has to have a subject.

**`be` plus a past participle.** The form test, and it is not sufficient on its
own — _The driver was reliable_ has the same shape and is a subject complement.
That contrast with lesson 11 is available and unused.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                               | What defeats it                       | In the course?                  |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| _was_ plus an _-ed_ word means passive | _The building was unsafe_ — lesson 38 | **no**, not adjacently          |
| A passive has _by_ in it               | _Those deeds were filed_              | **yes** — 3 of 10 have no agent |
| The subject does the action            | this whole lesson                     | **yes** — finally               |

## What this should change

1. **Review the two readings of the proposed near-miss.** _The streets were
   deserted_ can name a state or a passive event. Keep it only if both readings
   are representable.
2. **Put an active beside its passive.** The corpus already has near-pairs across
   lessons 35 and 37 — _The ledger audited by the inspector vanished_ and _The
   ledger was audited by the inspector_ — and they are two lessons apart.
