## What the lesson decides

| Decision           | In plain words             |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- |
| `form:Conj`        | _and_, _but_, _or_         |
| `func:coordinate`  | one of the joined equals   |
| `func:coordinator` | the word doing the joining |

## The finding

**Every coordination in the lesson is two noun phrases in subject position.**
Measured: the only place a coordinate structure sits is `subject/NP`, ten times
out of ten. Nine use _and_; one uses _or_.

So the lesson shows one of the many things coordination can join, in one of the
places it can sit. Missing: coordinated adjectives (_calm and patient_),
coordinated prepositional phrases (_through the gate and across the field_),
coordinated objects, and any three-way list.

Three distinct asked-shapes, and verb-final in 5 of 10.

## The tests

**Coordination is itself a constituency test**, which is why this lesson is a
good place to look backwards. Only like joins to like: if two runs can be joined
by _and_ and the result is grammatical, they are the same kind of thing. That is
one of the standard tests from lesson 1, arriving as a topic in its own right.

**The joined parts can swap.** _The bread and the cheese_ / _the cheese and the
bread_. A modifier and its head cannot.

**Agreement changes.** _The bread and the cheese **vanished**_ takes a plural
verb where either alone would take a singular. This is the sharpest formal
evidence that the coordination is one subject and not two, and no sentence in
the lesson draws attention to it.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                            | What defeats it              | In the course?                  |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Coordination joins noun phrases     | adjectives, phrases, clauses | **no** — 10/10 are noun phrases |
| Coordination happens in the subject | an object, a complement      | **no** — 10/10                  |
| _and_ is the coordinator            | _but_, _or_                  | partly — one _or_, no _but_     |

## What this should change

1. **Join something other than a noun phrase.** Adjectives and prepositional
   phrases are both in scope and neither appears.
2. **Coordinate an object.** Ten subjects in a row makes position look like part
   of the definition.
3. **One three-way list.** _food, water, and blankets_ raises the comma question
   that lesson 39 will need.
