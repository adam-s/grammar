# Lesson 15 — The six types, one procedure · sentences

Drafted 28 August 2026. See [README.md](README.md).

**Status:** the table below is the live course text. Where this page measures a
corpus — shortcuts, counts, what was missing — it measures the one these
sentences replaced; the notes under the table describe what is built.

**This was already the best lesson in the course** — nine distinct asked-shapes
out of ten sentences, where twenty-one of the forty lessons managed two or fewer.
The changes here were small.

**The one thing it did not do:** no two sentences had the same shape and
different answers. Every type was distinguishable by counting noun phrases and
checking whether the last thing was an adjective.

## Notes

The lesson walks the frames in the order the verb adds demands: intransitive,
transitive, two objects, linking, _be_, a required adverbial, an object
complement, and last an object and a required place together. _They called her a
taxi_ sits inside that walk rather than after it, because it is where counting
noun phrases stops working — two objects that look exactly like an object and a
complement.

_They called her a taxi_ and _They called her a genius_ are where the procedure
becomes necessary: five words each, three noun phrases each, and only the _be_
test separates them.

_She put the letter on the desk_ is the last thing the stage has to offer, and it
has to survive the removal test that _The auditor questioned the figures_ does
not need — lesson 14's test arriving one more time.

Nothing here is longer than eight tokens. The hardest sentence in the lesson —
_They called her a genius_ — is one of the shortest, which is
[../difficulty.md](../difficulty.md)'s whole argument in one row.
