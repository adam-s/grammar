## What the lesson decides

**Nothing new.** `teaches` is empty. This is the lesson where everything from 8
to 14 has to be told apart without a heading to help.

## The finding: this is the best lesson in the course

**Nine distinct asked-shapes out of ten sentences.** No other lesson in the
course comes close. The next best is lesson 40 at seven, and the distribution
across all forty is lopsided: nine lessons ask for **one** shape ten times over,
twelve more ask for two, so **21 of 40 lessons have two or fewer**.

Picks range from 12 to 28, against lesson 14's 20 to 22. The lesson genuinely
varies, and it varies because the sentences were chosen to.

|                                               | Type                          |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| _Those negotiations collapsed._               | intransitive                  |
| _The auditor questioned the figures._         | transitive                    |
| _The explanation sounded plausible._          | linking                       |
| _That chairman was a banker._                 | _be_                          |
| _The foreman gave the apprentice a warning._  | two objects                   |
| _The inspector declared the building unsafe._ | object complement             |
| _The archive is below the stairs._            | required adverbial            |
| _She put the letter on the desk._             | object and required adverbial |

All seven frames, plus two more sentences reusing them. This is the procedure
the stage is named for, and it is the first place a learner has to run it.

## The one thing it does not do

**No two sentences have the same shape and different answers.** The types are
all present and all distinguishable by counting noun phrases and checking
whether the last thing is an adjective.

The pairs that would defeat counting are missing:

- _They called her a taxi._ (two objects) against _They called her a genius._
  (object complement). Five words each, three noun phrases each, and only the
  verb decides.
- _She kept the milk in the fridge._ (required) against _She drank the milk in
  the kitchen._ (optional).

Both pairs are exactly what lessons 12, 13 and 14 failed to give, and lesson 15
is where they belong.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                                   | What defeats it                             | In the course?                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Count the noun phrases                     | _called her a taxi_ / _called her a genius_ | **no**                                  |
| An adjective at the end means a complement | an adjective inside a longer object         | **no**                                  |
| The heading tells you the type             | this lesson                                 | **yes** — the lesson has no single type |

The last row is why this lesson works.

## What this should change

1. **Add the two matched pairs above.** Nothing else in the course tests the
   verb rather than the shape.
2. **Nothing else.** Lesson 15 is the model the rest of Stage 2 should copy, and
   the criticism of lessons 8 to 14 in these dossiers is essentially that they
   are not lesson 15.
