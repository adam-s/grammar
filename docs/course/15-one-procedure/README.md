## What the lesson decides

**Nothing new.** `teaches` is empty. This is the cumulative lesson where the
learner must choose among the verb-use labels already introduced in lessons
8–14, rather than use the lesson heading as the answer.

## Central generalization

**A verb use predicts the kinds of dependents that its clause needs. Read the
whole predicate, then use the relationship of each required phrase to the verb,
subject, or object to choose the course's label.** Counting words, noun phrases,
or final adjectives can locate a candidate, but cannot settle the analysis.

The title's **six types** is defensible only as the course's six _verb-use_
labels: `Vint`, `Vtr`, `Vlink`, `Vbe`, `Vg`, and `Vc`. The lesson also contains
seven _clause frames_, because a required adverbial is a function that can
complete either a _be_ use or a transitive use; it is not a seventh verb type.

| Course verb use            | Clause frame   | Live evidence                                                      |
| -------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| intransitive               | S V            | _Those negotiations collapsed._                                    |
| transitive                 | S V O          | _The auditor questioned the figures._                              |
| linking                    | S V C          | _The explanation sounded plausible._                               |
| _be_                       | S V C or S V A | _That chairman was a banker._ / _The archive is below the stairs._ |
| two-object                 | S V O O        | _The foreman gave the apprentice a warning._                       |
| object-complement          | S V O C        | _The inspector declared the building unsafe._                      |
| not an additional verb use | S V O A        | _She put the letter on the desk._                                  |

This taxonomy is a course choice, not a fact that all reference grammars state
the same way. The British Council groups _be_ with linking verbs; the course
gives it a separate label so learners can distinguish its subject-complement
and location uses. The page must name that convention rather than present it as
the only analysis of English.

## Relationships and diagnostics

The procedure works when it asks what phrases _do_, not merely what order they
occupy. An object is an NP directly licensed by a transitive use. In a
double-object construction, two NPs are separately licensed by the verb. A
subject complement predicates something of the subject; an object complement
makes a second predication about the direct object. A required adverbial
supplies a setting or other value that this verb use needs.

The live _called_ pair supplies the best contrast:

| Same visible shape          | Relationship                                                      |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _They called her a taxi._   | _her_ is the person for whom a taxi was called: two objects.      |
| _They called her a genius._ | _a genius_ is predicated of _her_: object plus object complement. |

Both clauses have three noun phrases and the same word order. The relationship,
not the count, decides them.

The current six-step procedure is defensible as a **scope-bound set of
converging checks** for the simple active declarative clauses in this stage. It
is not a single mechanical procedure for all English verb patterns.

1. Find the main verb and identify the phrases that follow it. Do not assume
   that every later phrase is an object.
2. If there is no required object, test whether any following adverbial is
   optional. An intransitive use may still have optional material after it.
3. With two adjacent post-verbal NPs, test their relationship. A related _to_
   or _for_ version supports a double-object reading; a natural _be_ or
   _become_ paraphrase supports an object-complement reading.
4. With a phrase after the verb, ask whether it predicates something of the
   subject, predicates something of an object, or supplies a setting the frame
   requires.
5. Use removal only on a candidate adverbial. If the intended frame remains
   grammatical, it is optional; if it is left unfinished, it is required.
6. Apply the course's separate `_be_` label after identifying its complement or
   required adverbial. The word _be_ alone does not determine that relationship.

| Check                  | What it supports                                      | Limit                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No direct-object NP    | an intransitive use                                   | An understood object or optional PP can make stopping early misleading.                                                                      |
| _to/for_ paraphrase    | many double-object readings                           | It is lexically restricted and may be a benefactive adjunct. In _called her a taxi_, the related wording changes to _called a taxi for her_. |
| _be/become_ paraphrase | an object or subject complement                       | Tense, aspect, and result meaning may require rewording; it is not word insertion.                                                           |
| Removal                | whether a candidate adverbial is required in this use | Less information is not the same as an ungrammatical or unfinished frame.                                                                    |
| Word order or NP count | where to look                                         | Neither distinguishes S V O O from S V O C.                                                                                                  |

The method fails outside its stated scope: clauses with omitted objects,
passives, questions, fronting, dummy subjects, coordination, particles,
prepositional verbs, non-finite or content-clause complements, and many
lexically selected PPs need additional analysis. A verb spelling can also enter
several frames. Say “classify this use,” not “learn what type this verb is.”

## Common summaries and corpus audit

| Summary                                       | What it gets right                          | What it omits or gets wrong                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| “There are six sentence types.”               | The course uses six verb-use labels.        | The live set has seven clause frames, including S V O A.                                         |
| “A verb has one type.”                        | Some verbs favour one frame.                | The classification belongs to a use in a clause, and one spelling can enter more than one frame. |
| “The noun after the verb is the object.”      | Ordinary S V O clauses often look that way. | Complements, PPs, clauses, and a second object occupy post-verbal positions too.                 |
| “Two nouns after the verb means two objects.” | It identifies the double-object order.      | _They called her a genius_ has the same order but an object complement.                          |
| “Put _is_ between the last two phrases.”      | It can expose a predicative relation.       | The paraphrase may need _become_ or rewording, and it does not identify an indirect object.      |
| “Remove the last phrase.”                     | It can test a candidate adverbial.          | It cannot identify a complement, and some removable phrases still carry important information.   |

The live practice set covers every course verb-use label and all seven clause
frames in the table. Items 4 and 9 are a controlled `V NP NP` pair that defeats
noun-phrase counting. Item 9 makes an NP, not merely a final adjective, an
object complement; item 10 shows that a transitive use can require both an
object and a location.

| Shortcut                                      | What the live set shows                                 | What it does not yet show                                                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| “Every post-verbal PP here is required.”      | _below the stairs_ and _on the desk_ are both required. | No optional PP control appears, so removal never rejects that answer.                                              |
| “The procedure always finishes in six steps.” | The short simple clauses suit the listed checks.        | No passive, question, particle, prepositional verb, clausal complement, or omitted-object case tests the boundary. |

The set is a good review of the course's simple frames, not a complete inventory
of English verb complementation.

## What the research should change

1. **Open with the scope.** Define a verb frame as the required pattern in a
   particular use, then say that this lesson reviews the six course labels and
   their seven clause-frame outcomes.
2. **Use the live _called_ pair as the main contrast.** A purpose-built,
   non-graded fixture would be better than exposing two practice answers;
   otherwise mark them as demonstrated. The current `fix-vg` / `fix-vc-noun`
   contrast teaches the principle but does not make counting fail as directly.
3. **Rewrite the procedure as conditional evidence.** Remove “find the main
   verb and stop there,” and do not make the _to/for_, _be/become_, or removal
   checks decisive in every construction.
4. **Make the six-types/seven-frames distinction visible.** Explain why S V A
   and S V O A are required-adverbial frames, rather than quietly adding a
   seventh verb type.
5. **Add one optional-PP control when practice next changes.** _The auditor
   questioned the figures in the archive_ leaves a complete S V O frame after
   removal. Keep _She put the letter on the desk_ as the required S V O A
   contrast.
6. **Do not claim a universal classifier.** Put the limits beside the procedure,
   especially the course-specific treatment of _be_ and the lexical limits on
   the paraphrases.

## Sources opened and read

- Anderson et al., _Essentials of Linguistics_, second edition, [6.2, “Word
  order”](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/chapter/a-starting-point-word-order/).
  Read in full. It treats transitivity or valency as the number of arguments a
  predicate takes, distinguishes intransitive, transitive, and ditransitive
  predicates, and distinguishes structural grammatical roles from event roles.
  Its three-way account supports the lesson's starting point but does not make
  the course's six labels a universal taxonomy.
- British Council LearnEnglish, [“Clause structure and verb
  patterns”](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/clause-structure-verb-patterns).
  Read in full. It states that verbs select different clause patterns, includes
  transitive, intransitive, linking, and double-object examples, and separately
  notes infinitival, _-ing_, and content-clause patterns omitted from this
  lesson's scope.
- Department for Education, [_English glossary_](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7c8e4ded915d48c24108e2/English_Glossary.pdf).
  Read in full. Its school-grammar entries explain complements as adding
  information to a subject or object, say that _be_ normally has a complement,
  and explicitly acknowledge that grammar terminology can vary by framework.
  These are the familiar summaries the page needs to qualify.

Cambridge English Grammar Today's pages on objects, complements, subject
complements, and verb patterns were located. The publisher returned a 403 when
their full pages were opened from this research environment, so this dossier
does not claim to have read them or rely on them as evidence.

## Rejected

- **A universal six-step classifier.** The checks are useful in their stated
  construction; they do not cover English verb complementation as a whole.
- **Noun-phrase counting.** The live _called_ pair now proves that it fails.
- **A seventh verb type for required adverbials.** It confuses a required clause
  function with the course's verb-use labels.
- **Treating the course's _be_ label as uncontested grammar.** It is a useful
  course convention that needs to be named as such.
