# Lesson 38 — Interjections and sentence-edge words

Researched 28 August 2026. An author's dossier. See
[../01-introduction/README.md](../01-introduction/README.md).

## What the lesson decides

| Decision          | In plain words                                       |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `form:Interj`     | an interjection                                      |
| `func:supplement` | it sits outside the sentence frame and fills no slot |

## The finding

**`form:Interj` is taught here and appears in none of the 400 sentences.**

The ten sentences are all evaluative adverbs: _Unfortunately, Surprisingly,
Naturally, Regrettably, Predictably, Curiously, Frankly, Happily, Apparently,
Sadly._ Not one _Oh_, _Well_, _Hey_ or _Ouch_.

So the lesson's title names two things and delivers one. `form:Interj` is one of
exactly two decisions in the whole course that are taught and never used; the
other is `aux:do` at lesson 24.

That is worth fixing in one direction or the other. A decision the palette will
accept and no sentence exercises is a claim the course has not earned.

Four distinct asked-shapes, which come from the varying main clauses rather than
from the edge material.

## The tests

**Removal leaves a whole sentence.** _That ferry sank._ This is what
`func:supplement` means: it fills no slot, so taking it out breaks nothing.

**It cannot be questioned or fronted into the frame.** _\*How did that ferry
sink? Unfortunately._ An adverbial answers a question; a supplement does not.
This is the test that separates lesson 38 from lesson 18, and the two are
otherwise easy to confuse because both are often single adverbs.

**Position is free.** _That ferry, unfortunately, sank._ / _That ferry sank,
unfortunately._ A supplement can go almost anywhere; an adverbial cannot.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                                         | What defeats it                                 | In the course? |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| The supplement is the first word and has a comma | a medial or final supplement                    | **no** — 10/10 |
| A supplement is an _-ly_ adverb                  | an interjection, a noun phrase                  | **no**         |
| Every sentence-initial adverb is a supplement    | _Yesterday the children played_ is an adverbial | **no**         |

The third row is the real risk. Lesson 18's adverbs are adverbials; lesson 38's
are supplements; both can start a sentence with a comma. Nothing in either
lesson puts the two side by side, and the removal test does not separate them —
both drop. The test that does is the question test above.

## What this should change

1. **Use an interjection, or stop teaching `form:Interj`.**
2. **Move a supplement into the middle or the end.** Free position is the
   clearest evidence of what a supplement is.
3. **One matched pair with lesson 18.** _Sadly, the crew left_ against
   _Yesterday, the crew left_. Same shape, different function.
