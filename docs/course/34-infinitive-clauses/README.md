[../01-introduction/README.md](../01-introduction/README.md).

**Status:** This dossier measures the built corpus. [sentences.md](sentences.md) proposes replacements that are not yet parsed or accepted as course data.

**Page contract:** The learner-facing lesson will be a static, standalone visual explanation under [the shared lesson contract](../../lesson/README.md). This dossier supplies its answer, tests, contrast, and common confusion; it is not learner copy or an interaction script.

## What the lesson decides

| Decision           | In plain words                            |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| `part:infinitival` | the _to_ is a particle, not a preposition |
| `fin:infinitival`  | the clause has no tense                   |

The first decision is the sharp one. _to_ in _to renew_ is not the _to_ in _to
the bank_, and lesson 25 has already taught that a small word can belong to the
verb instead of taking a complement.

## The finding

**All ten are the same construction.** _The tenant wanted to renew the lease.
The board agreed to fund the repair. She hoped to finish the survey._

Subject, verb, _to_, verb, object. Two distinct asked-shapes.

**No infinitive clause anywhere in the course has its own subject.** Checked
across all 400 sentences: _asked the driver to wait_, where the waiting is done
by the driver and not by the asker, does not exist. Nor does an infinitive as
subject (_To leave would be rude_), nor one inside an adjective phrase (_eager to
help_), nor a purpose infinitive (_stopped to rest_).

The missing subject matters most. Without it, the learner has no evidence that
an infinitive clause is a **clause** at all — every example has an invisible
subject that happens to match the main one, so _to renew the lease_ looks like
part of the verb phrase.

## The tests

**`to` will not take a noun phrase.** _to renew_ against _to the bank_. If a
noun phrase can follow, it is a preposition; if a bare verb must, it is the
infinitive particle.

**No tense.** Lesson 3's test comes back: shift the sentence in time and _to
renew_ does not change. _wanted_ does. That is what `fin:infinitival` records.

**Substitution.** The whole clause is the object of _wanted_: _The tenant wanted
**it**_.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                                  | What defeats it                        | In the course?           |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| _to_ plus a verb is an infinitive         | true, and it is the whole test         | fine                     |
| The infinitive has no subject of its own  | _asked the driver to wait_             | **no** — 0 in the course |
| An infinitive clause is always the object | subject position, adjective complement | **no**                   |

## What this should change

1. **One infinitive with its own subject.** It is the evidence that this is a
   clause, and the course has none.
2. **One infinitive that is not an object.** Either position would do.
