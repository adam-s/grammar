## What the lesson decides

| Decision  | In plain words                                     |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `vt:Vint` | this use of the verb has no direct-object function |

This is the first of the course's six verb-frame choices. The label belongs to
the verb as used in one clause, not permanently to its dictionary spelling.

## The central generalization

**An intransitive use of a verb has no direct object.** The subject and verb can
form the core clause without a noun phrase filling an object position:

> _The engine stalled._

That does not mean that nothing may follow the verb:

> _She smiled at us._
>
> _The old bridge collapsed overnight._

The PP and adverb add information to an intransitive clause without becoming
direct objects. “The verb stands alone” is therefore a memorable title, not the
structural definition.

The lesson should also distinguish grammatical frame from conversational
completeness. A hearer may want to know why the engine stalled, but that does
not create a missing object. Conversely, context can leave an understood object
unspoken after a normally transitive use: _Have you eaten?_ Meaning that feels
complete is useful evidence only when the structural alternatives are
controlled.

## Classify the use, not the spelling

English verbs vary in how many frames they enter. Sometimes a verb occurs with
or without an object while retaining much the same meaning:

- _I already ate._
- _I already ate lunch._

Sometimes the change in frame changes the relationship among participants:

- _The hatch opened._
- _She opened the hatch._

In the second pair, the intransitive subject undergoes the change; the
transitive subject causes it and the object undergoes it. The live set plants
two such alternations: _The hatch opened_ connects to transitive _open_ in
lesson 9, while _The tide turned_ connects to linking _turn_ in lesson 10.

The course stores `Vint`, `Vtr`, or another frame on the verb token in the
accepted sentence parse. The learner page is right to say “this use.” It should
not imply that every occurrence of _turn_, _open_, _eat_, or _leave_ has one
fixed type.

## Object absence and material after the verb

The grammar model licenses no direct-object sibling under a `Vint` predicate.
It does license adverbials. That yields the relationship the learner must see:

```text
predicate
├── head: intransitive V
└── optional adverbial or other non-object material
```

At lesson 8 scope, the course has not yet introduced the `direct object` label
or the phrase labels used by most adverbials. The page can still show that
_stalled_ has no object slot, but it cannot teach every possible post-verbal
function in the same diagram. _She smiled at us_ belongs in practice because it
defeats the positional shortcut even while its PP waits for later analysis.

## The available evidence

### The no-object frame

The direct evidence in _The engine stalled_ is the accepted frame: the VP has a
verb head and no direct-object dependent. A matched transitive clause in lesson
9 will make the contrast stronger because a noun phrase after a verb is not an
object merely by position.

Asking “verb what?” can expose a bad object candidate in clear examples:
_What did the baby sleep?_ does not produce the object of _slept_. But this is
a classroom prompt, not an analysis. It works poorly with understood objects,
idioms, and verbs whose transitive and intransitive senses differ.

### Addition

Adding a noun phrase is useful only when the basic verb meaning is held steady:

- _The baby slept._ / *_The baby slept the blanket._
- _Several guests arrived._ / *_Several guests arrived the station._

The current worked example behaves differently:

- _The engine stalled._
- _The engine stalled the traffic._

Both are grammatical, but _stall_ has changed frames and causal structure. The
pair demonstrates why an intransitive label belongs to a use, not why the word
_stall_ rejects objects. The learner page already notices the different use;
its test should not present addition as a simple grammaticality verdict.

### Omission

Stopping at the verb can reveal a self-contained intransitive frame in _The
engine stalled_. It cannot by itself distinguish an intransitive use from a
transitive use with an omitted or understood object. _She ate_ and _She read_
are possible in suitable contexts.

The test is strongest with a controlled sentence whose accepted interpretation
does not supply an understood patient. It is not the definition of
intransitivity.

### Passive

A canonical direct object can normally become the subject of a related passive:

> _Maya opened the hatch._ → _The hatch was opened by Maya._

The intransitive _The hatch opened_ has no object to promote. This is strong
supporting evidence for the difference, but passive voice arrives at lesson 37.
Not every transitive clause has an equally natural passive, so passivization is
not a universal definition either.

## Common summaries and their limits

| Summary                                      | What it captures                               | What it misses                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “takes no object”                            | the defining frame contrast used by the course | the label applies to a use, and later analysis must identify what counts as an object |
| “needs nothing after it”                     | simple clauses such as _The baby slept_        | adverbials and PPs can follow an intransitive verb                                    |
| “makes a complete sentence”                  | the felt completeness of clear examples        | conversational completeness and syntactic complementation are not the same            |
| “cannot answer verb what?”                   | a quick rejection in _sleep what?_             | omitted objects and alternations make the prompt unreliable                           |
| “appears at the end”                         | the old two-word corpus                        | five live examples continue after the verb                                            |
| “an intransitive verb never takes an object” | strictly intransitive lexemes                  | many verb lexemes have both transitive and intransitive uses                          |

The author-level summary is about the clause frame:

> In an intransitive use, the verb licenses no direct object. Other material may
> follow, and the same verb lexeme may license a different frame in another
> clause.

## Current corpus audit

The pre-conversion set put the verb last in all ten sentences and gave no verb a
contrasting use elsewhere. The live set fixes both defects:

- five sentences continue after the verb;
- _She smiled at us_ puts a noun phrase after a preposition without creating a
  direct object of _smiled_;
- adverbs such as _overnight_, _loudly_, and _completely_ show optional
  post-verbal material;
- _open_ returns transitively in lesson 9;
- _turn_ returns as a linking verb in lesson 10.

The set now defeats “the verb must be final,” “nothing can follow an
intransitive verb,” and “a verb has one permanent type.” It does not yet display
a direct-object label, because that is precisely lesson 9's new decision.

| Shortcut                               | Current evidence                                 |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| The sentence stops at the verb.        | Five predicates continue.                        |
| Any noun after the verb is its object. | _She smiled at us_ places _us_ inside a PP.      |
| Each verb has one type.                | _open_ and _turn_ recur in different frames.     |
| Two words means intransitive.          | Most live sentences contain more than two words. |

## What the research should change

1. **Define the frame by absence of a direct object.** Remove “or complement”
   and avoid treating conversational completeness as the definition.
2. **Replace “Stop at the verb” as the main heading.** The live corpus correctly
   includes material after intransitive verbs.
3. **Use _stall_ as an alternation, not a failed addition.** _The engine stalled
   the traffic_ is grammatical in a different frame and makes the token-level
   classification visible.
4. **Bring one continuing predicate into the explanation.** _She smiled at us_
   proves that post-verbal position does not create a direct object.
5. **Keep the _turn/open_ forward connections only where they prove the
   generalization.** The close should state that verbs license different frames,
   not advertise later lessons.
6. **Carry the passive contrast to lesson 37.** It becomes useful evidence only
   after the course can explain promotion and voice.

## Rejected

- **“Nothing follows an intransitive verb.”** The live lesson itself contains
  PPs and adverbs after the verb.
- **“A complete thought needs no object.”** Semantic satisfaction and
  grammatical object structure are different questions.
- **A permanent list of intransitive verbs.** English commonly uses one lexeme
  in several frames.
- **Addition as a yes/no test without controlling sense.** _Stall_ and _open_
  gain grammatical transitive uses rather than merely becoming ill-formed.
- **Passive as the lesson-8 procedure.** It is strong later evidence but outside
  the current label scope and not exceptionless.
