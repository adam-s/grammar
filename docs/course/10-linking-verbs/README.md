## What the lesson decides

| Decision                 | In plain words                         |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| `form:Adj`               | this word is an adjective              |
| `form:AdjP`              | this run is an adjective phrase        |
| `func:subjectComplement` | it describes the subject, not the verb |
| `vt:Vlink`               | the verb links them                    |

Four decisions, the largest single jump in Stage 2.

## The tests

**Substitute _be_.** This is the standard test and it is reliable:

> replacing the verb with a form of "be" … "The flowers smell wonderful" becomes
> "The flowers are wonderful," which makes sense and confirms it's a linking verb

_The soup tasted salty_ → _The soup was salty_. It holds. _The mechanic replaced
the belt_ → _\*The mechanic was the belt_. It does not.

**Adjective, not adverb.** After a linking verb the word describes the subject,
which is a noun, so it takes the adjective form. _The soup smells good_, not
_\*smells well_. This is the most common real-writing error in the area and it
falls straight out of the structure.

**The complement points backwards.** A direct object is a different thing from
the subject; a subject complement is the same thing. _The room seemed empty_ —
_empty_ is the room.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                              | What defeats it       | In the course?                              |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| The complement is an adjective        | _He is a doctor_      | **no** — all ten complements are adjectives |
| Sense verbs are linking               | _She tasted the soup_ | **no**                                      |
| The word after the verb is the object | this whole lesson     | yes, that is the lesson                     |

Two distinct asked-shapes. Every one of the ten is `NP V Adj.`

**No noun-phrase subject complement appears in this lesson at all.** Those are
held back for lesson 11, so a learner leaving lesson 10 has seen the subject
complement only as an adjective.

And no verb here appears in its transitive sense. _feel_, _turn_, _grow_, _taste_
are all in the lesson and all one-sided. _The milk turned sour_ is here; _The
tide turned_ is in lesson 8. Two lessons apart, never compared.

## What this should change

1. **Give one verb two senses inside the lesson.** _The children grew._ / _The
   farmer grew potatoes._ / _The evening sky grew dark._ One verb, three types,
   four words each. This is the cheapest real difficulty in Stage 2.
2. **The adjective-not-adverb rule deserves a hint.** It is the point at which
   this analysis stops being an exercise and starts fixing writing.
