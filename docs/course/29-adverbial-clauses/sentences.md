# Lesson 29 — Adverbial clauses · sentences

Drafted 28 August 2026. See [README.md](README.md).

**The shortcut:** every adverbial clause in the built lesson comes **after** the
main clause. Ten of ten. Position is most of what makes an adverbial clause hard
to find, and it is the one thing the lesson holds constant.

The marker inventory is the built lesson's strength — _because, after, until,
when, once, whenever_ — and it is kept.

## Sentences

| #   | Sentence                                           | The step                                  |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| 1   | We waited because the gate was locked.             | a reason clause, after the main clause    |
| 2   | The ferry waited because the tide turned.          | the same marker, a different frame        |
| 3   | She waved when the last bus arrived.               | a time clause                             |
| 4   | Because the gate was locked, we waited.            | **item 1, fronted** — and a comma appears |
| 5   | When the last bus arrived, she waved.              | **item 3, fronted**                       |
| 6   | The room darkened until the fire caught.           | a boundary in time rather than a point    |
| 7   | The lamp flickered before the power failed.        | the clause names what came after          |
| 8   | Before the power failed, the lamp flickered.       | **item 7, fronted** — three pairs now     |
| 9   | The crew cleared the track after the storm passed. | a longer main clause on both sides        |
| 10  | Those shutters rattled whenever the wind rose.     | a marker of repetition                    |

## Notes

Items 1/4, 3/5 and 7/8 are three controlled pairs: the same words in two
positions. Fronting is the definitive test that a clause is an adverbial rather
than a complement, and the built lesson never uses it.

**The commas in items 4, 5 and 8 are the point of the pairs, not decoration.**
Lesson 39 exists to say punctuation is evidence and currently has one punctuation
pattern; a fronted adverbial clause is the second, and it comes from here.

The removal test also works and is worth naming: _We waited._ survives. Unlike
lesson 14, this lesson has something for it to reject — an adverbial clause is
always optional, so the contrast is with the nominal clause of lesson 28, which
cannot be removed.

**The marker cannot be dropped.** _\*The ferry waited the tide turned_ is not a
sentence, and that separates `func:marker` here from lesson 28's zero-marked
nominal clause, where nothing is there to drop.
