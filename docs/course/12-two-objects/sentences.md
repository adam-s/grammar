# Lesson 12 — Two objects · sentences

Drafted 28 August 2026. See [README.md](README.md).

**Status:** the table below is the live course text. Where this page measures a
corpus — shortcuts, counts, what was missing — it measures the one these
sentences replaced; the notes under the table describe what is built.

**The measured shortcut:** in all ten sentences this replaced the indirect object
was definite and the direct object began with _a_. Looking for the article scored
ten out of ten, and the _to_/_for_ paraphrase was never needed.

The test is that paraphrase: _handed the guest a key_ → _handed a key **to** the
guest_. The thing that can move behind a preposition is the indirect object.

## Sentences

## Notes

_The clerk showed a visitor the map_, _He wrote his sister the letter_ and _The
porter brought the guests their cases_ break the article shortcut three different
ways. Once all three are in, _a_ tells the learner nothing.

_The porter handed the guest a key_ and _The porter carried the heavy cases_ are
a near-pair on one subject: two objects, then one. _The nurse brought a blanket_
is unpaired, so the count is not decided by noticing a twin.

**_The guide gave a map to us_ is the important one.** _gave a map to us_ has the
same words as _gave us a map_ and a different tree — one object and an adverbial,
not two objects. It is the paraphrase test made into a sentence, and it is what
stops a learner deciding that "three noun phrases" is the rule.

The pair that separates this lesson from lesson 13 belongs at lesson 15: _They
called her a taxi_ against _They called her a genius_.
