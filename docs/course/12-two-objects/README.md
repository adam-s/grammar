## The tests

**The _to_ / _for_ paraphrase.** This is the defining test:

> An indirect object can generally be converted into a prepositional phrase
> (with to and for) and placed after the direct object (e.g., "She gave a gift
> to him").

_The porter handed the guest a key_ → _handed a key to the guest_. The thing
that can move behind a preposition is the indirect object.

**The passive.** An indirect object can become the subject: _The guest was
handed a key_. Lesson 13 is where this test earns its keep, because an object
complement cannot do it.

**Order.** The indirect object comes first when there is no preposition. That is
a fact about position, not a test, and it is the shortcut below.

## Shortcut register

| Shortcut                                                    | What defeats it                                   | In the course? |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| The first noun phrase after the verb is the indirect object | _They elected the lawyer their chair_ — lesson 13 | **no**         |
| Three noun phrases means two objects                        | lesson 13, same shape                             | **no**         |
| **The one with _a_ is the direct object**                   | a definite direct object                          | **no** — 10/10 |

The third row is measured and it is the sharpest shortcut found anywhere in
Stage 2. **In all ten sentences the indirect object is definite and the direct
object begins with _a_:**

| Indirect     | Direct     |
| ------------ | ---------- |
| the guest    | a key      |
| the couple   | a mortgage |
| the children | a story    |
| his sister   | a postcard |

…and six more. A learner who never learns the paraphrase can score ten out of
ten by looking for the article.

Two distinct asked-shapes across the lesson.

## The confusion this lesson sets up and does not resolve

_The porter handed the guest a key_ and _The members elected the lawyer their
chair_ have **the same surface shape**: noun phrase, verb, noun phrase, noun
phrase. One is two objects and one is an object and its complement.

They are in different lessons, so the learner is never asked to choose. Lesson
15 mixes the types but does not put a matched pair in front of anyone.

## What this should change

1. **Vary the determiners.** The _a_/_the_ split is a free pass and costs
   nothing to remove.
2. **One sentence that is not ditransitive.** _The porter carried the heavy
   cases_ beside _The porter brought the guest a key_ forces the count.
3. **The matched pair with lesson 13** belongs in lesson 15: _They called her a
   taxi_ / _They called her a genius_.

## Rejected

- **"The indirect object is the receiver."** Semantic, and it fails on _The
  charity sent the village a pump_, where the village receives nothing in any
  ordinary sense, and on _taught the squad a routine_.
