# Lesson 24 — Auxiliary verbs · sentences

Drafted 28 August 2026. See [README.md](README.md) and the optional companion
[../24a-what-the-helper-means/](../24a-what-the-helper-means/).

**Status:** the table below is the live course text. Where this page measures a
corpus — shortcuts, counts, what was missing — it measures the one these
sentences replaced; the notes under the table describe what is built.

**Two measured gaps, both now closed.** `aux:do` was taught here and appeared in
**none** of the 400 sentences this replaced — one of exactly two decisions taught
and never used. And **no sentence anywhere had more than one auxiliary**, so the
ordering rule (modal, then perfect, then progressive) was invisible. Two of the
rows below use _do_ and two stack a pair of auxiliaries.

_do_ matters more than a missing example. It is the auxiliary English inserts
when a sentence needs one and has none, which is what proves the category exists.

## Sentences

| #   | Sentence                                | The step                                         |
| --- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | The visitors will wait.                 | a modal — the frame at its clearest              |
| 2   | The visitors have waited.               | perfect, everything else held still              |
| 3   | The visitors are waiting.               | progressive, everything else held still          |
| 4   | The visitors did wait.                  | **_do_**, which the course has never used        |
| 5   | That engine was failing.                | progressive with _be_, not _have_                |
| 6   | Our guests have been waiting.           | **two auxiliaries**, perfect then progressive    |
| 7   | The clerk did file the deeds.           | _do_ again, with an object, so it is not a fluke |
| 8   | A mechanic may have checked the brakes. | **two auxiliaries**, modal then perfect          |
| 9   | The board should approve the plan.      | a modal in a transitive frame                    |
| 10  | She has repaired the gate.              | perfect in a transitive frame                    |

## Notes

_The visitors will wait_, _The visitors have waited_ and _The visitors are
waiting_ hold the subject and verb completely still and change only the
auxiliary. That is the correct way to show a paradigm, and the corpus this
replaced already did it.

_Our guests have been waiting_ and _A mechanic may have checked the brakes_ are
the chains. **No fixture proved two auxiliaries in one verb phrase** — nothing
forbade it either, and `VP > Aux/auxiliary` exists, so it was representable
rather than a gap. Both are built and reachable now, which settles it.

**The inversion test is what an auxiliary is.** _The tide had risen_ → _Had the
tide risen?_, where _The tide rose_ → _\*Rose the tide?_ fails. Questions are
Course 2 material, so the test cannot be run inside a sentence here — but it is
the reason _do_ exists and the reason _The visitors did wait_ and _The clerk did
file the deeds_ belong.

This is also the first lesson where lesson 3's tense test has a competitor: in
_That engine was failing_, the word that changes with the time is _was_.
