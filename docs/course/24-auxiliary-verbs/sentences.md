# Lesson 24 — Auxiliary verbs · sentences

Drafted 28 August 2026. See [README.md](README.md) and the optional companion
[../24a-what-the-helper-means/](../24a-what-the-helper-means/).

**Two measured gaps.** `aux:do` is taught here and appears in **none** of the 400
sentences — one of exactly two decisions in the course that are taught and never
used. And **no sentence anywhere has more than one auxiliary**, so the ordering
rule (modal, then perfect, then progressive) is invisible.

_do_ matters more than a missing example. It is the auxiliary English inserts
when a sentence needs one and has none, which is what proves the category exists.

## Sentences

| #   | Sentence                                  | The step                                         |
| --- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | The visitors will wait.                   | a modal — the frame at its clearest              |
| 2   | The visitors have waited.                 | perfect, everything else held still              |
| 3   | The visitors are waiting.                 | progressive, everything else held still          |
| 4   | The visitors did wait.                    | **_do_**, which the course has never used        |
| 5   | The clerk did file the deeds.             | _do_ again, with an object, so it is not a fluke |
| 6   | She has repaired the gate.                | perfect in a transitive frame                    |
| 7   | The engine was failing.                   | progressive with _be_, not _have_                |
| 8   | The mechanic may have checked the brakes. | **two auxiliaries**, modal then perfect          |
| 9   | Our guests have been waiting.             | **two auxiliaries**, perfect then progressive    |
| 10  | The board should approve the plan.        | close on a modal, in a fuller frame              |

## Notes

Items 1, 2 and 3 hold the subject and verb completely still and change only the
auxiliary. That is the correct way to show a paradigm and the built lesson
already does it.

Items 8 and 9 are the chains. **No fixture proves two auxiliaries in one verb
phrase** — nothing forbids it either, and `VP > Aux/auxiliary` exists, so this is
probably representable rather than a gap. It should be checked before the
sentences are written, and if it fails it is a model finding, not a sentence one.

**The inversion test is what an auxiliary is.** _The tide had risen_ → _Had the
tide risen?_, where _The tide rose_ → _\*Rose the tide?_ fails. Questions are
Course 2 material, so the test cannot be run inside a sentence here — but it is
the reason _do_ exists and the reason items 4 and 5 belong.

This is also the first lesson where lesson 3's tense test has a competitor: in
_The engine was failing_, the word that changes with the time is _was_.
