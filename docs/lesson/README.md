# How other people teach this

Researched 27 August 2026, after the note that "a lesson" is the wrong unit.
Five survey documents sat here and were deleted once their conclusions were
applied; this is what they found. Git has the surveys if the sourcing is ever
worth re-checking.

## The finding

**A "lesson" bundles three things the research says to separate:** the
explanation, the order, and the practice. Every format that measures well pulls
them apart.

**Isolated grammar teaching is the one writing intervention that measures
negative.** Graham and Perin's 2007 review found traditional grammar teaching
was the only practice with a negative effect; Andrews and colleagues found no
benefit in 2006. That does not condemn this app, which is not a writing
intervention. It condemns the shape: a unit built around a label, practised in
a block, is the exact thing that was measured and found wanting.

**What measures well is never a lesson.** Sentence combining scores 0.5 to 0.7.
Data-driven learning puts the learner in front of real corpus lines and asks
them to find the pattern. Duolingo's Smart Tips fire *after* a specific mistake
rather than before, and learners who see them make fewer later errors.
Interleaved practice beats blocked practice widely. None of these is a page you
read before you are allowed to try.

**Reference works have no lessons and cover more ground.** Cambridge splits the
noun phrase across five consultable entries. Englicious ships starters,
projects, games and glossary items rather than a sequence.

## What it changed

Keep the dependency graph in [../course/README.md](../course/README.md); drop
the five-part lesson container. The palette already does the thing the evidence
supports: it explains at the moment of the decision, in the learner's own half-
built sentence, and never before they are allowed to try.

## Sourcing, honestly

Read in full: Language Log on grammar in schools, Grammar Pedagogy on subjects
and predicates, Nicky Case on explorable explanations, Wikipedia on Reed-Kellogg
diagrams, the Form-Function Method article.

Search summaries only, because the source blocked automated reading: the UK
National Curriculum English glossary, the UKLA primary grammar report, the
Essentials of Linguistics chapters, Englicious itself, the WAC Clearinghouse
grammar chapter. Anything attributed to those is second-hand and should be
checked before it is quoted to a learner.
