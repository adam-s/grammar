## The short version

**The instinct is correct, and the evidence is stronger than expected.** A
"lesson" bundles three things that the research says should be separated: the
explanation, the order, and the practice. Every format that measures well pulls
them apart.

Three findings drove that conclusion.

**Isolated grammar teaching is the one writing intervention that measures
negative.** Graham and Perin's 2007 review of writing instruction found
traditional grammar teaching was the only practice with a negative effect.
Andrews and colleagues found no benefit in 2006. This does not condemn the app,
because the app is not a writing intervention, but it does condemn the shape:
a unit built around a label, practised in a block, is the exact thing that was
measured and found wanting.

**What measures well is never a lesson.** Sentence combining scores 0.5 to 0.7.
Data-driven learning puts the learner in front of real corpus lines and asks
them to find the pattern. Duolingo's "Smart Tips" fire after a specific mistake
rather than before, and learners who see them make fewer later errors.
Interleaved practice beats blocked practice by a wide margin. None of these is
a page you read before you are allowed to try.

**Reference works do not have lessons, and they cover more ground.** Cambridge
splits the noun phrase across five consultable entries. Englicious ships
starters, projects, games, and glossary items rather than a sequence. You look
things up when you need them.

The recommendation in [suggestions.md](suggestions.md) is to keep the
dependency graph that `../course/README.md` already worked out, drop the
five-part container, and replace it with named units that each do one job.

## What was read directly, and what was not

Honesty about sourcing, because it changes how much weight each claim carries.

**Read in full:** the Language Log post on grammar in schools, the Grammar
Pedagogy post on subjects and predicates, Nicky Case on making explorable
explanations, the Wikipedia article on Reed-Kellogg diagrams, and the
Form-Function Method article.

**Available only as search summaries**, because the source blocked automated
reading or returned unreadable output: the UK National Curriculum English
glossary, the UKLA primary grammar report, the Essentials of Linguistics
chapters, Englicious itself (the site was unreachable during this research),
and the WAC Clearinghouse grammar chapter. Quotations attributed to these are
second-hand and should be checked against the source before any of them is
quoted to a learner.
